## 1. The Historical Trap: How a Repudiated Theory Became a Coercive Cult (Document 1A)

When the supply-side revolution was launched under Ronald Reagan's *Economic Recovery Tax Act of 1981* (and imported into New Zealand a decade later via the **1991 *Mother of All Budgets*** and the **Employment Contracts Act 1991**), it did not arise from a consensus in academic economics. On the contrary, the mainstream econometric profession—and its own architects—repudiated its core claims in real time:

1. **Immediate Repudiation by Economists and Insiders (1980–1982):**
   * **Arthur Laffer's Napkin vs. Empirical Elasticities:** When political polemicists claimed that cutting the top marginal tax rate from 70% to 50% (and later 28%) would *increase* federal tax revenues, MIT's **Paul Samuelson** and **Robert Solow**, Harvard's **Martin Feldstein** (who served as Reagan's own Chairman of the Council of Economic Advisers from 1982 to 1984), and **Alan Blinder** demonstrated that actual taxable income elasticities ($\varepsilon \approx 0.15\text{–}0.25$) placed advanced economies far to the left of the Laffer peak.
   * **The "Trojan Horse" Admission:** In December 1981, Reagan's Director of the Office of Management and Budget, **David Stockman**, confessed to *The Atlantic* that the administration's supply-side revenue model was rigged and that the Laffer curve was merely a *"Trojan horse to bring down the top rate"*—deliberately engineering a structural fiscal deficit ("Starve the Beast") to force future cuts to Medicare, education, and social assistance.
   * **The Chamley–Judd Misuse:** In theoretical macroeconomics, orthodox defenders cited **Chamley (1986)** and **Judd (1985)** to claim that the optimal long-run tax on capital is zero ($\tau_k^\star = 0$). Thirty-four years later, **Straub and Werning (2020, *American Economic Review*)** proved that the Chamley–Judd result was a mathematical artifact of assuming infinite intertemporal substitution by dynasties and non-binding debt limits; whenever workers face borrowing constraints and saving elasticities are realistic, the optimal long-run capital tax is **strictly and substantially positive**.

2. **The Perverse Behavioral Contradiction (Carrots for Rentiers, Coercion for Workers):**
   * As **John Kenneth Galbraith** observed, supply-side dogma rests on a bizarre psychological inversion: **the wealthy will not work or invest unless they are given *more* money** (lavish tax cuts, zero capital-gains/land taxes), whereas **wage-dependent workers will not work unless they are given *less* money** (benefit cuts, punitive sanctions, union de-certification, and healthcare/housing precarity).
   * In New Zealand, the **1991 Employment Contracts Act** and **1991 Benefit Cuts** operationalized this coercive control: stripping collective bargaining, cutting core benefits by up to 25%, and imposing 70%–100% effective marginal abatement rates (`EMTRs`) on the working poor. Rather than unleashing a productivity miracle, New Zealand's labor productivity growth fell behind the OECD median while household debt and urban land prices (`3.1x` income) exploded.

3. **The 50-Year Empirical Verdict (Hope & Limberg, 2022):**
   * Using causally identified synthetic-control methods across **18 OECD countries over 50 years (1965–2015)**, **David Hope and Julian Limberg (2022, *Socio-Economic Review*)** proved that major tax cuts for the rich produce **zero statistically significant effect on real GDP growth or unemployment** ($\Delta g_Y \approx 0$), while permanently increasing the top 1% income share by **+0.8 percentage points** inside five years.

### 3.1 The Piketty–Saez–Stantcheva (2014) Three-Elasticity Refutation of the Laffer Curve

Orthodox supply-side models attribute the entire observed elasticity of top taxable income ($e_{\text{total}}$) to real productive labor/entrepreneurial supply ($e_1$). **Piketty, Saez, and Stantcheva (2014, *AEJ: Economic Policy*)** prove that $e_{\text{total}}$ is the sum of three distinct elasticities:

$$e_{\text{total}} = \underbrace{e_1}_{\text{Real Productive Supply }\approx 0.15} + \underbrace{e_2}_{\text{Tax-Sheltering / Reclassification}} + \underbrace{e_3}_{\text{Zero-Sum Bargaining Extraction}}$$

When top tax rates $\tau_{\text{top}}$ are cut without closing land/capital shelters (`H:05`), executives and rentiers increase aggressive compensation bargaining ($e_3 > 0$) at the direct expense of median wage-earners ($-\Delta w_{\text{median}}$). Because the social welfare weight on zero-sum rent extraction is negative, the revenue-maximizing and welfare-optimal top tax rate is far above post-1981/1991 statutory rates, and cutting $\tau_{\text{top}}$ unambiguously reduces fiscal revenue:

$$\frac{d\mathcal{R}}{d(1 - \tau_{\text{top}})} = Y_{\text{top}} \left( e_1 - \frac{1 - \tau_{\text{top}}}{\tau_{\text{top}}} \right) < 0 \quad \text{whenever } e_1 \approx 0.20 \ll \frac{1 - \tau_{\text{top}}}{\tau_{\text{top}}} \approx 1.50$$
